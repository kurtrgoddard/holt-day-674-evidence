1 · What this series claims — and refuses to claim

The series estimates current standing (a recency-weighted polling benchmark), stress-tests an election held now (deterministic seat scenarios), and audits mandate conversion (promises → outcomes → recognition). It deliberately does not publish a 2028 re-election probability: the forecast gate (§8) fails. Fair-mindedness is procedural here, not asserted: symmetric party treatment, visible uncertainty, published disconfirming evidence, consistent comparison dates, labelled claim types, a public corrections log, and named unknowns.

Every factual assertion carries a claim ID (C01–C39) mapped to source, workbook cell and status in the claim ledger. Claim types: verified fact · calculation · scenario · inference · hypothesis · non-finding. Forecasts would be labelled “forecast” — none appear in this edition. Namespaces: public claim IDs C01–C39 form the PUB-C series (C ≡ PUB-C); the conversion workbook’s internal audit rows are the separate AUD-C series. Source IDs are CONV-Sxx (conversion workbook) and D674-Sxx (day-674 workbook).

2 · Plain-language definitions

Percentage point: the simple difference between two percentages. 43% → 33% is a fall of 10 points (not “10 percent”).
Nowcast: an estimate of what would happen if the election were today. It says nothing about 2028.
Reference interval: the range the Liberal–PC lead plausibly sits in given how much the three polls disagree — a diagnostic, weaker than a classical confidence interval.
Support-retention ratio: the party’s current poll share divided by its own election share. Holt’s ≈89% describes support kept so far — it is not a probability of anything.

3 · Clocks and baselines

Primary clock: days after the October 21, 2024 election — August 26, 2026 is day 674. Sensitivity clock: days after the November 2, 2024 swearing-in (day 662). Ordinary election date: October 16, 2028 under the Legislative Assembly Act, RSNB 2014, c 116, s 3(3)–(6) — 782 days after cutoff — subject to dissolution discretion (D674-S04; consolidated text verified 2026-08-26). Vote shares use valid votes for cross-election comparability (the official popular-vote page divides by all ballots and prints slightly lower figures). Historical inputs are frozen at each comparator’s own day-674 date; later-fielded polls are excluded.

4 · Poll synthesis

Inclusion: independent province-wide NB vote-intention releases with fieldwork midpoints within ~90 days of the cutoff — exactly three qualify (Porter O’Brien, Narrative, Abacus). Weighting: weight = 0.5^(age/90), ages from exact field-midpoint half-days; normalized weights 0.267 / 0.297 / 0.436. Result: L 43.154 / PC 32.965 / G 13.449; lead 10.189. Respondent bases are not interchangeable and are preserved: Porter O’Brien decided voters; Narrative a derived decided normalization of rounded all-eligible values (party denominator 69; effective n≈342 approximate); Abacus decided voters with an undisclosed decided base (total n=601 used for variance — an optimistic assumption).

Interval: DerSimonian–Laird between-poll variance on the L–PC margin (τ≈3.8 pts) plus nominal multinomial variance → ensemble SE ≈3.177 → 90% reference interval +4.963 to +15.415 (published as +5.0 to +15.4). Not a classical CI: all three surveys are non-probability or mixed panels; τ is unstable at n=3. No margins of error are assigned to opt-in panels. Published “other” shares on each decided basis: Porter O’Brien 5; Narrative 15.94 (derived, 11/69); Abacus 10 (residual; published values sum to 99 from rounding). All-respondent undecided values (21 / 27) sit in a separate column and are never mixed with decided denominators.

Half-life sensitivity (lead): 11.7 at 30 days · 10.6 at 60 · 10.2 at 90 (headline) · 10.0 at 120. Logged discrepancy: the source report’s text prints 11.8 at 30 days; the workbook computes 11.736. Graphics use the 90-day benchmark only. Excluded stale waves (weight 0): Abacus Oct 2025 (lead 24), Porter O’Brien Nov 2025 (9), Angus Reid Nov 2025 (17).

5 · Seat benchmark

Each party’s certified 2024 vote in each of the 49 ridings is scaled by the ratio of its target three-party share to its 2024 three-party share; the highest projected index wins the riding (strict-greater tie rule; boundary searches by bisection to 1e-6, reported at three decimals). This proportional swing preserves Green concentration better than additive swing but remains deterministic: no candidates, incumbency, turnout correlation, regional shocks or riding-level error. Outputs are scenarios — 26 L (Porter O’Brien) / 30 L (Narrative, derived) / 32 L (Abacus) / 30-17-2 (benchmark) — never probabilities. Riding inputs and an executable recomputation harness ship in the replication package (data/ridings_2024_three_party.csv + code/build.py). Ledger reconciliation: 162 votes for a withdrawn NDP candidate (Saint John Portland–Simonds) are excluded, matching the official 374,661 valid votes.

6 · Firewall and stress routes

Under the same swing model, the majority is lost at 8.237 points of adverse L–PC margin erosion (public rounding 8.2; sixth loss Kent North, which flips Green) and a PC seat plurality arrives at 11.370 (11.4; Saint John Portland–Simonds). From the weakest current poll (Porter O’Brien, 26 seats) the majority is only 2.925 further points away. Four ecological routes reach the same boundaries (direct switching 4.1 / turnout withdrawal 7.3 / non-voter recruitment 9.1 / Liberal→Green 5.7 per-100 magnitudes; plurality 5.7 / 9.3 / 11.5 / 9.3) — stylized aggregates, not voter-flow estimates. A plurality does not by itself determine government formation. Both boundaries are stored as values in the workbook; code/build.py recomputes them from the riding CSV and prints any deviation.

7 · Historical comparison rules

Latest qualifying poll on or before each day-674 date; question types kept separate; offsets and grades disclosed rather than window-robustness asserted: Graham (May 2008 CRA value; exact field dates NOT FOUND; A− retrospective table), Alward (−71 days; B), Gallant (−15 days; A− archived), Higgs (−68 days; A; NB decided n=199; polls disagree by 9 points that month), Holt (−23.5 days to latest component). Margin drifts to the next election: −29.3 / −20.1 / −16.1 for the three full-term cases; −8.0 for Higgs’s 47-day runway. Trajectory cones at 3–24 months: not estimable — no curve is interpolated through unpolled periods. Boundaries and assembly size changed 2010→2014 and before 2024, so historical seat counts never calibrate the 2024 map.

8 · The forecast gate

A 2028 probability would require: verified current polls → comparable measures → an adequate historical training set → successful hindcasts → calibration. The chain fails at stage 3: only three usable scheduled-term NB endpoints exist (Higgs is a non-exchangeable snap-election case), the historical intermediate record is not model-grade, and no ≈30-mandate Canadian training set exists at primary-source vintage standard (scope searched: CRA, MQO, Angus Reid and Narrative public archives plus NB Legislative Library election records, 2006–2026 — the non-estimability claim is scoped to this dataset and this evidence standard, not to all conceivable data). Benchmark hindcasts on the four cases (MAE, all|ex-Higgs): election carry-forward 9.8|10.6 · day-674 poll carry-forward 9.6|12.7 · leave-one-out share drift 6.6|3.8 — internally cross-validated on only four cases, with three training cases per fold; this is not external validation. The Higgs-excluded folds have only two training cases each (Higgs removed from both training and scoring; exact value 3.848130, superseding the workbook’s mis-sliced 4.423) and cannot validate a forecast. Withdrawn: state-space model, Canadian hierarchical pooling, Monte Carlo seat simulation. The PC leadership election (October 17, 2026) also post-dates the cutoff, so the opposition configuration itself is unobserved.

9 · Promise, outcome and flagship rules

The 2024 platform was atomized into 104 canonical units (102 raw bullets → 108 atomic rows → 4 duplicates/cross-references retained but not double-counted). Official tracker labels are preserved separately from the analyst-coded public-evidence stage; most stage inputs originate in government trackers, so the coding is analytical, not an independent evaluation. Five flagships were pre-selected (prominence, fiscal scale, salience, expected lag) and tracked along promise → funded action → implementation → service output → resident outcome → public recognition. Stages are never collapsed into a score; spending, announcements and labels are never described as outcomes; “not yet observed” is a data status, never converted to amber or red; no causal attribution without a credible counterfactual; policy areas are never ranked by arbitrary weights.

10 · Communications and administrative-health rules

The descriptive contrast: implementation indicators coexist with low public evaluations. Credit conversion and weak mandate compression are labelled hypotheses with stated disconfirming tests (linked awareness → attribution → adequacy → experience measurement). Contrary evidence is published alongside: wildfire response earned the government’s highest issue rating, and available broad indicators do not support a French-language transmission failure (reception itself unmeasured; the rural-Francophone cell is n=97). Senior civil-service morale is coded NOT ESTABLISHED in public evidence — the absence of any representative series triggered measurement design, not a finding; individual accounts are never presented as survey results. Objective strain mechanisms (12% Part I reduction plan, RTIPPA 59.5% timeliness, an Auditor-General staffing case, dual-hatting) justify amber on capacity, not on morale.

11 · Chart integrity rules applied

Bar axes start at zero; the one non-zero baseline (slope chart) is explicitly marked “0 = tied”; no 3-D, no area/volume encoding of 1-D values; regional subsamples shown as descriptive text only; every seat graphic shows all 49 seats and the 25-seat line; uncertainty shown wherever the model produces it; unpolled periods drawn as gaps, never interpolated; public figures rounded consistently (one decimal for points; exact values retained here and in the workbook); correlation never described as cause; party colour appears only on party data, and series are distinguished by direct labels and position, not colour alone.

12 · Accessibility

Contrast meets WCAG 2.2 AA on the off-white paper: primary ink #22262B 13.96:1 and secondary #5A6069 5.82:1 on #F7F5EF; amber #8A5E0D reserved for large or bold type; greys on dark cards at #9BA1A8 or lighter. Minimum in-frame type ≈13–14 px source on 1350 masters after the v1.1 floor raise (qualifiers ≥16 px), with dense frames flagged for optional split editions; Atkinson Hyperlegible chosen for character disambiguation; expanded party names on first use per frame set; alt text and a plain-text transcript for every asset ship in the copy + alt-text file; reading order in exports follows the visual top-to-bottom order; no maps with tiny labels; no decorative chart junk.
